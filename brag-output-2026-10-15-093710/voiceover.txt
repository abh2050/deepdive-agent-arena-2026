A citation can look convincing and still fail to support the sentence beside it. DeepDive checks the evidence before it publishes the claim.

It is a self-verifying research analyst built for Agent Arena twenty twenty-six. Ask an open question, and it produces a report whose claims remain traceable to captured source text.

A LangGraph coordinator plans the work. Researchers gather web evidence. A claim writer proposes atomic statements. Then a separate verifier decides what survives.

The gate is deliberately strict: the quoted text must exist verbatim in the stored excerpt, and an independent Mesh-routed model must confirm that it entails the claim.

DeepDive decomposes the question, searches, captures evidence, challenges claims, and retries when coverage is thin.

This result is not optimized for a flattering score. Unsupported or ambiguous candidates are rejected. Only verified claims enter the deterministic report, with coverage and pass-rate metrics exposed.

Click any citation and the evidence becomes inspectable: the original URL, the captured excerpt, the exact quote, its quality signal, and the verifier's decision. That is claim-level provenance, not a decorative bibliography.

The audit trail records the process too: planning, searches, evidence, verdicts, retries, and final metrics, so a judge can inspect how the answer was made.
